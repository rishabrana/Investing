If we channel Warren Buffett, Charlie Munger, and Mohnish Pabrai, the focus is on intrinsic value, moats, management quality, and margin of safety. These investors care less about short-term “market” metrics and more about long-term business fundamentals that reveal economic strength and cash-generation durability.

⸻

🧭 Top 20 Metrics for a Value Investor

(Grouped by Buffett-style investing logic)

⸻

🏦 1. Intrinsic Value vs Market Price
• Definition: Estimated fair value (via DCF or owner earnings) compared with the current market price.
• Why: Determines your margin of safety.
• Formula idea:
\text{Margin of Safety} = 1 - \frac{\text{Market Price}}{\text{Intrinsic Value}}

⸻

💵 2. Free Cash Flow (FCF)
• Definition: Cash generated after capex; true cash earnings.
• Buffett’s favorite because “earnings are an opinion, cash is a fact.”
• Trend over 5–10 years shows sustainability.

⸻

📈 3. Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
• Formula:
ROIC = \frac{NOPAT}{Invested Capital}
• Measures how efficiently capital produces returns.
• Buffett seeks > 10–12% consistently.

⸻

💰 4. Return on Equity (ROE)
• Shows shareholder return efficiency.
• Compare 10-year average vs peers — high and stable is ideal.

⸻

📉 5. Debt-to-Equity & Interest Coverage
• Prefer low leverage and easy coverage.
• \text{Interest Coverage} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest Expense}}
• Munger quote: “The first rule of compounding is to never interrupt it unnecessarily.”

⸻

🔁 6. Earnings Per Share (EPS) Growth
• Long-term compounded EPS growth (≥ 10 yrs) signals durable profit engines.

⸻

🧱 7. Book Value Per Share Growth
• Reflects retained earnings and reinvestment quality.

⸻

🧮 8. Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
• Use forward P/E relative to growth and history.
• Buffett dislikes “paying up,” but will for durable moats.

⸻

🧾 9. Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio
• Useful for financials, energy, and asset-heavy businesses.
• Compare with ROE to find undervaluation.

⸻

📊 10. Enterprise Value / EBITDA (EV/EBITDA)
• Capital-structure-neutral valuation multiple.
• Buffett and Munger prefer using EV instead of market cap.

⸻

📉 11. PEG Ratio (P/E ÷ Growth Rate)
• Useful sanity check for growth vs price.
• PEG < 1 = potential undervaluation.

⸻

📦 12. Operating Margin & Net Margin
• Stable or expanding margins show pricing power — a hallmark of a moat.

⸻

🏗 13. Owner Earnings
• Buffett’s version of free cash flow:
\text{Owner Earnings} = \text{Net Income} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Other Non-Cash Charges} - \text{Capex}
• Reflects true distributable cash.

⸻

🧍 14. Management Return on Retained Earnings
• Measures how well management reinvests profits.
• Buffett: “For every dollar retained, make sure you’re getting more than a dollar of market value.”

⸻

🛡 15. Economic Moat Indicators
• Qualitative but measurable via:
• High gross margins
• Stable market share
• Recurring revenues
• Low customer churn
• Brand intangible assets

⸻

⏳ 16. Consistency of Performance
• Year-over-year stability of earnings, ROE, margins, and cash flow.

⸻

🧩 17. Capital Expenditure Ratio
• \text{Capex / Operating Cash Flow}
• Lower ratios mean high capital efficiency — more cash for shareholders.

⸻

📈 18. Dividend Yield and Payout Ratio
• A steady or growing dividend with a payout < 60% is healthy.
• Reinvested dividends compound wealth over time.

⸻

📉 19. WACC vs ROIC Spread
• Positive spread = value creation.
• The larger the spread, the stronger the economic moat.

⸻

🧠 20. Ten-Year Average Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)
• Smooths out cycles and shows sustained efficiency.

⸻

🧰 Buffett–Munger Framework Summary

Category Key Metric Target / Philosophy
Profitability ROIC, ROE, Margins > 10%, stable
Cash Generation FCF, Owner Earnings Positive & growing
Financial Strength Debt/Equity, Coverage Low debt, > 5× coverage
Valuation P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, DCF Below intrinsic value
Management Quality Return on Retained Earnings > market average
Durability Consistent Growth & Moat Sustainable advantage
Shareholder Return Dividend + Buyback Yield Rising over time
